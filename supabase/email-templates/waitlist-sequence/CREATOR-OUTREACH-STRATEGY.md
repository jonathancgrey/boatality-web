# Boatality Creator Outreach Strategy
## Zero-budget. Personal. 10 creators before users see a single page.

---

## The Core Principle

You're not running a campaign. You're having 10–20 conversations.

Every single creator you contact should feel like you found *them specifically* and reached out because of something real you saw in their content. That's only possible at small scale — and small scale is exactly where you are. Use it.

The goal isn't a big waitlist. The goal is **10 creators with published content on the platform before a single user gets access.** Users should open Boatality and find a world already alive. Not a ghost town.

---

## The 5 Phases

```
Phase 1: Find them         (your list of ~30–50 targets)
Phase 2: Reach out         (personal DMs / emails)
Phase 3: They sign up      (landing page → waitlist)
Phase 4: You approve them  (fast — within 48hrs)
Phase 5: They publish      (onboard → create → content live)
         ↓
       Open to users
```

---

## Phase 1: Finding Creators (Zero Budget)

**What you're looking for:**
- Real boat people, not lifestyle influencers cosplaying as sailors
- Consistent publishers — at least 10–20 pieces of content, not 3 posts from 2022
- Engaged comment sections (even if small audience) — this signals community
- Any format: YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, podcast, blog
- Audience size: **500 to 50,000** is the sweet spot. Big enough to be serious, small enough to care deeply about a new platform built for them.

**Where to find them:**

| Platform | How to search |
|----------|--------------|
| YouTube | "liveaboard", "sailing vlog", "offshore fishing", "boat build", "cruising", "marina life", "boat restoration" — sort by upload date, not views |
| Instagram | #liveaboard #sailinglife #boatlife #fishingguidetips #marinebiologist #bluewater #passagemaking |
| TikTok | #boatlife #sailingcouple #fishingcharter #liveaboardlife — focus on people posting consistently |
| Podcasts | Search Apple Podcasts / Spotify for "sailing", "fishing", "boating" — hosts are ideal creator types |
| Reddit | r/sailing, r/liveaboard, r/Fishing — find people who write long, knowledgeable posts. Those are writers. |
| Forums | Cruisers Forum, The Hull Truth, Sailing Anarchy — same thing. People who write well in forums often want a real publishing outlet. |
| Facebook Groups | "Liveaboard Community", "Sailing Families", "Blue Water Cruising" — look for people who post consistently and get engagement |

**Content categories to target (for variety on launch):**
- Offshore / bluewater sailing
- Fishing guides / charters / tournament fishing
- Liveaboard life (especially families)
- Boat builds and restorations
- Marine biology / ocean content
- Kayak / paddle / coastal
- Cruising destinations
- Boating education / seamanship

**Build a simple tracking list (spreadsheet):**

| Name | Platform | Channel/Handle | Content type | Audience size | Outreach sent | Signed up | Approved | Publishing |
|------|----------|---------------|-------------|--------------|--------------|-----------|----------|-----------|

Aim for 30–50 targets. Expect 30–40% to respond, and 50% of those to actually go through with it. You need 10 publishers, so have a pipeline of at least 30.

## Phase 2: The Outreach Message

**The rules:**
1. Never send a template that sounds like a template
2. Reference something specific — a video title, an episode, a post
3. One paragraph. No pitch decks. No attachments.
4. Ask for nothing except a reply

**DM template (adapt per person):**

> Hey [name] — I've been watching your content for a while. [One specific thing you liked — a specific episode/video/post].
>
> I'm building a platform called Boatality — specifically for boat creators. Not another YouTube or Substack, but a place designed around how boat content actually works: long-form, slow-build, real audiences. Still early but we're opening it to a first group of creators before we go public.
>
> I thought of you because [specific reason related to their content / style]. Would you be open to hearing more?
>
> — Jonathan

**What to fill in:**
- "[One specific thing]" = "your episode on crossing the Azores — the part about the weather window decision was exactly the kind of thing that gets buried on YouTube"
- "[specific reason]" = "you write about boat restoration in a way that actually teaches people, and that's rare"

**Email version** (if you can find their email — check their YouTube About tab or website):

> Subject: Boatality — I think you'd be a good fit for this
>
> [Same as above, slightly expanded — 3 short paragraphs max]

**What to do when they reply:**

Just talk to them like a person. Answer their questions. If they're interested, send them the landing page link and ask them to sign up. Don't over-explain. Let the conversation be a conversation.

## Phase 3: The Signup → Approval Loop

Once they sign up on the landing page, **approve them within 24–48 hours.**

They said yes to a personal invitation. Making them wait 2 weeks feels like a bait and switch. The waitlist emails in this sequence are for the organic signups — people who found the site themselves. Your personally outreached creators should move fast.

**In the admin panel:**
- See them appear in beta_signups as "pending"
- Click approve → invite email fires → they set password → onboard

## Phase 4: The Waitlist Email Sequence (Organic Signups)

These emails are for people who found Boatality themselves and signed up without a personal invitation. The tone is still warm and founder-personal, but it acknowledges they found you rather than you finding them.

> **Important:** Creators you personally invited should NOT sit in this sequence long. Approve them quickly. The sequence below is for organic waitlist sign-ups, or for personally-invited creators if there's a delay on your end.

### Email 1 — Immediate (Subject: "You're in. Welcome to the first wave. ⚓")
*See: 01-confirmation.html*

Key message: You're part of a deliberate small rollout. When you get access the app will still be pre-launch — you're helping shape it, not just using it.

### Email 2 — Day 4 (Subject: "Why this matters")
*See: 02-the-story.html*

Key message: Jonathan's personal story about why the platforms weren't built for boat people. Ends with "You're not just early. You're the reason this exists."

### Email 3 — Day 10 (Subject: "What you'll actually build")
*See: 03-inside-the-studio.html*

Key message: Features framed around what creators build, not what the tool does. Ends with a question: "What's the first thing you'd publish if everything was built for you?" — because replies from organic signups help Jonathan understand who to prioritise.

### Email 4 — Day 21 (Subject: "Tell me what you create")
*See: 04-holding-your-spot.html*

Key message: Honest check-in. Waiting is annoying. Here's where things are. Asks them to reply and tell him what they create — this is the filter that helps identify serious creators from the organic list.

## Phase 5: Keeping Approved Creators Warm While You Wait for 10

Once a creator is approved and onboarded, they're in the platform but users aren't yet. This is the most important phase to get right — you don't want them publishing two posts and then going quiet because nothing's happening.

### Creator Status Update Emails (post-onboarding)

**Update 1 — 1 week after onboarding**
Subject: "Here's who's joining you"

> Short email. Tell them how many other creators are onboarded. Name a couple (with permission). Make them feel like part of a founding group, not a solo test. Ask them to publish their first piece if they haven't — "it doesn't have to be perfect, it just needs to be yours."

**Update 2 — When you hit 5 creators**
Subject: "Halfway there — and it's looking good"

> Milestone update. The platform is filling up. Describe what content is starting to live there (without giving away specific creators if they'd prefer privacy). Tell them users are coming soon. Build anticipation.

**Update 3 — When you're ready to open to users**
Subject: "The gates open [date]. Your audience is coming."

> This is the launch moment. Tell them the exact date users will get access. Give them a week's notice so they can publish something fresh right as people arrive. Make them feel like the reason people are showing up.

---

## The Launch Sequence

When you're ready to open to users:

1. Send the **"Gates open" email** to all creators (1 week before)
2. Send a **"We're opening"** broadcast to the full organic waitlist (same day as open)
3. Open the floodgates in the admin panel — start approving users

The users who arrive should find:
- At least 10 creator channels with profile and branding set up
- At least 1–3 pieces of content per creator (so ideally 15–30 pieces total)
- A platform that feels alive, not empty

## Realistic Timeline

| Week | Focus |
|------|-------|
| 1–2 | Build your target list (30–50 creators). Start DMs. |
| 2–4 | Conversations happening. First 5–10 signups rolling in. Approve fast. |
| 3–6 | Creators onboarding. Publishing first content. Status updates keeping them warm. |
| 6–8 | Hit 10 creators with content. Run final check. Open to users. |

This is aggressive but doable. The bottleneck is almost always creator onboarding speed, not finding people.

---

## What Could Go Wrong (and how to handle it)

**"I'm too busy right now"** → "No problem — I'll keep your spot. Just reply when you're ready." Don't push. Keep the door open.

**Creator onboards but never publishes** → Send them the "what's the first thing you'd publish" email directly. Sometimes people freeze because it feels like a big deal. Tell them it doesn't need to be perfect.

**You're at 8 creators and stuck** → Go back to your outreach list and send 10 more DMs. Also consider: do any of the 8 know a creator friend they'd recommend?

**Creator churns before launch** → It happens. That's why you're targeting 30+ outreaches to get 10 publishers. Build in buffer.
